The one thing that makes this song click

Every chord is played on the top four strings only — D, G, B and high E. The bottom two are never sounded.

That's what lets the right hand stay completely still: your thumb owns the D string on every chord, and i–m–a sit on G, B and E permanently. One finger per string, never moving. The picking is identical from the first bar to the last — all the work is in the left hand.

The picking pattern

Six notes per bar, up and back down: p–i–m–a–m–i. Counted as 1 2 3 4 3 2, where the numbers are strings, not beats.

  • Anchor your pinky on the body below the strings. It stabilises the hand so the three fingers can move independently.
  • The thumb note is the loudest. Everything else is lighter.
  • Keep it perfectly even before you speed it up — an uneven roll is far more noticeable than a slow one.

Left hand

  • F xx3211 and G xx5433 are the same shape two frets apart. Learn one, slide it.
  • Am xx7555 is the only barre: index flat across G, B and E at the 5th fret, ring finger on D at the 7th.
  • Em xx2000 is one finger — index on the D string, 2nd fret, everything else open.
  • Press with the tips of your fingers so each one touches only its own string. A finger leaning onto a neighbour is the usual cause of a dead or buzzing note.

Capo & singing it

Shapes never change with a capo — everything moves up together. Use the capo control to find a comfortable place for your own voice.

Bunyan sings very high and light. If you're lower, stay at capo 0 and drop an octave rather than fighting for her register.
