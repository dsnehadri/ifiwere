How to practise this

  • Start at 60–70% tempo. The whole song lives on six open chords; the difficulty is in keeping the right hand even, not in the shapes.
  • Loop one section at a time with the loop button on each section header.
  • The bass note matters more than anything: Dm→D string, C and Am→A string, Em, F, G→low E.
  • Let notes ring into each other. Don't damp between chords — the overlap is the sound.
  • Play it quieter than feels natural. Bunyan's records are close-miked and very soft; a hard attack kills the character.

Making F easy

The barre F is the only hard shape here, and you almost certainly don't need it. Use Fmaj7 xx3210 instead — it keeps the open B and high E ringing, which suits the fingerpicking far better than a barre.

Switch the library above to see both shapes. If you want more bottom end, Fadd9 xx3213 also works.

Capo & singing it

The shapes never change when you use a capo — everything just moves up. The capo control raises the pitch so you can find a comfortable place for your own voice.

Bunyan sings very high and light. If you're singing lower, try capo 0 and drop the octave rather than fighting for her register.

Accuracy — please read

The chord progression comes from a published transcription and is corroborated by the song's key: every chord is diatonic to C major, which is consistent across sources.

The fingerpicking patterns are idiomatic suggestions I built to fit the harmony — they are not a verified transcription of the record. No source I found notates the right hand.

The timing assumes two beats per chord. Play along with the recording and switch to 4 beats if a section drifts.
